About

Research at the interface of engineering and medicine

I am a researcher in the Department of Anesthesiology at Weill Cornell Medicine. My work focuses on computational methods for extracting clinically useful information from physiological and wearable data, with particular interest in perioperative care, cardiorespiratory fitness, ECG/EEG analysis, and interpretable machine learning.

My background spans medical physics, mechanical engineering, physiological signal processing, and data-driven modeling. I am especially interested in methods that are technically rigorous, clinically meaningful, and transparent enough to support real-world decision making.

Research

Research Areas

Wearable Digital Health

Modeling activity, heart rate, sleep, HRV, and recovery trajectories from wearable sensors to support perioperative assessment and longitudinal monitoring.

ECG & EEG Analytics

Developing computational pipelines for physiological waveform processing, phenotyping, transportability, and outcome prediction.

Interpretable Machine Learning

Building prediction models with careful validation, feature attribution, domain-level interpretation, and clinically meaningful error analysis.

Perioperative Outcomes

Studying preoperative fitness, postoperative recovery, length of stay, and physiologic signatures associated with surgical outcomes.
