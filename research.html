Research

Digital Health & Computational Physiology

My research develops clinically interpretable computational methods for physiological signals, wearable data, and perioperative decision support.

Core Areas

Perioperative Digital Health

Data-driven approaches for preoperative assessment, intraoperative physiological monitoring, postoperative recovery, and clinically meaningful risk stratification.

Wearable Sensing

Analysis of activity, heart rate, HRV, sleep, and recovery trajectories from wearable devices to characterize cardiorespiratory fitness and functional status.

ECG & EEG Analytics

Signal-processing and machine-learning pipelines for electrocardiography and electroencephalography, including phenotyping, neurological assessment, and explainable prediction.

Interpretable Machine Learning

Model development with careful validation, error analysis, feature attribution, transportability assessment, and domain-level interpretation for clinical applications.

Research Approach

From physiological measurement to clinical interpretation

My work typically begins with a clearly defined clinical or engineering question, followed by physiological data curation, feature extraction, model development, subject-aware validation, explainability analysis, and interpretation of failure modes.

I am particularly interested in models that remain understandable enough to support scientific interpretation and clinical decision-making rather than functioning only as black-box predictors.
